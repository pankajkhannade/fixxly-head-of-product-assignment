# Fixxly market & contractor economics — research brief

**Purpose:** Ground Part 1 before locking *Agla Phase Kit*. Answers: TAM, job types, who pays for materials, small vs multi-day work, and what that implies for retention features.

**Sources:** Public reports, competitor sites, government stats, industry articles. All links in footnotes. **No Fixxly launch data** — sizing uses assignment context (3 dark stores, ~60 OPD/store, 30 min, Bangalore) + [HomeRun](https://home-run.co/) as live comparator.

---

## Executive summary (read this first)

1. **India materials TAM is enormous** ($27B+ construction materials; $265B+ infra + real estate construction spend). **Fixxly’s wedge is a tiny, high-velocity slice:** urgent / same-day material runs to **trade contractors** in dense metro pockets — not “all construction.”

2. **Contractors are not one persona.** A mistri doing **3 leak jobs today** (new site each time, customer-funded materials) behaves totally differently from a team doing **one 21-day bathroom** (phased BOM, repeat buys). **Agla Phase Kit only fits the second world** — and possibly a **minority of orders** at launch.

3. **Who pays:** Often the **homeowner/client** pays for materials (directly or via contractor quote); contractor is **procurement agent + labour**. On small jobs, materials are **job-specific and non-repeating on Fixxly** — retention is about **being default urgent channel**, not “next phase.”

4. **No clean national stat** for “% jobs &lt;1 day vs &gt;1 day.” Proxies suggest **repair/maintenance is huge and recurring in frequency**, but **renovation/multi-day captures more material ₹ per project**. Fixxly’s 30-min model skews toward **urgent, smaller baskets** early.

5. **Before Part 1.2:** Segment contractors and **don’t assume phase-based retention** for the whole base. Consider **two products:** (A) *Urgent today* — same-day reorder, favourites, site receipt; (B) *Agla phase* — only for **active multi-day jobs**.

### 1.2 SAM — “Quick building materials to contractors” (Bangalore / metros)

**Definition:** Trade buyers (plumber, electrician, mistri, small contractor) who would buy **non-bulk, job-urgent** materials with **&lt;2 hour** delivery from a dark store.

**Demand drivers:**

- Bangalore = **most saturated quick-commerce city** (~153 sq ft dark store / 1000 people vs ~51 India avg) — habit for speed exists ([Moneycontrol / ICICI Sec](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/startup/bengaluru-emerges-as-india-s-most-saturated-quick-commerce-hub-with-3x-average-dark-store-density-icici-securities-13923215.html))
- Post-pandemic: **labour 35–40% of build cost**, skilled labour scarce — **waiting on site = expensive** ([Construction Week](https://www.constructionweekonline.in/business/navigating-residential-costs-in-indias-post-pandemic-market))
- **EY building products:** Fragmented dealer network, inconsistent price/availability → room for **tech distribution** ([EY PDF](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/advanced-manufacturing/documents/ey-the-ascending-trajectory-unveiling-the-swift-growth-of-the-building-products-market-in-india.pdf))

**SAM sizing (illustrative, Bangalore):**

| Input | Assumption | Rationale |
|-------|------------|-----------|
| Urban plumbing / MEP workforce | **~0.8–1.5M** in Karnataka plausible slice of **4.2M** India informal plumbers ([TOI / IPSC cite](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/skilled-worker-shortage-poses-challenge-to-governments-major-png-expansion-drive/articleshow/131276949.cms)) | 90% informal, on-the-job trained |
| Active “buy materials on phone” subset | **10–25%** of urban trades | Digitally ready, metro, urgent jobs |
| Bangalore active base (SAM users) | **~40k–120k** | Order-of-magnitude, not census |
| Orders / active user / month | **4–12** if Fixxly becomes habit | vs 1–2 if only emergency |
| AOV urgent materials | **₹800–₹4,000** small job; **₹5k–₹25k** phase | Plumbing cost guides ([Building & Interiors](https://buildingandinteriors.com/plumbing-cost-in-india-2026-price-breakdown-services-material-rates/)) |

**SAM revenue (Bangalore, annual, rough):**

`50k active buyers × 6 orders/mo × ₹2,500 AOV × 12` ≈ **₹9,000 Cr GMV** theoretical upper — **realistically 1–5% capturable** in early years = **₹90–450 Cr** GMV if execution works.

## 2. Value chain — who buys, who pays, who decides

```
Homeowner / builder (pays)
        ↓
Contractor / mistri / plumber (specs + procures + installs)
        ↓
Dealer / WhatsApp / Fixxly / HomeRun (fulfills materials)
        ↓
Brand / distributor
```

### 2.1 Who pays for materials?

| Scenario | Who pays | Contractor role | Fixxly implication |
|----------|----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Home repair** (leak, tap, blockage) | Homeowner (material + labour bill) | Buys on behalf; may mark up | **Urgent small basket**; new “job” daily; client money |
| **Renovation** (bathroom, kitchen) | Homeowner / builder budget | Plans phases; procures per phase | **Multi-day same site**; phased materials |
| **Small contractor on rental flip** | Landlord / investor | Single procurement window | Medium basket, tight timeline |
| **Large contractor / developer** | Company treasury | Bulk, credit, project PO | **Not Fixxly v1** — [1Supply](https://1supply.in/), [BuilditIndia](https://builditindia.com/), [Infra.Market](https://infra.market/), [McCoy Mart credit](https://mccoymart.com/credit-finance/) |

**GST nuance (residential):** “Labour + material” contracts often **18% GST**; “materials only” path has different ITC logic — pros use **work contracts** ([Construction Week](https://www.constructionweekonline.in/business/navigating-residential-costs-in-indias-post-pandemic-market)). Contractor may **bundle** material in quote — homeowner doesn’t see Fixxly brand.

**Urban Company model (services, not materials):** Customer pays **visiting charge + service**; plumber dispatched in ~90 min ([Urban Company](https://www.urbancompany.com/near-me/plumbers-near-me)). Materials often **separate** — plumber or customer sources locally. Fixxly sits **material layer under** this ecosystem, not replacing UC.

---

### 2.2 Who decides channel?

| Decision maker | Channel bias |
|----------------|--------------|
| **Lead mistri** (expert) | Dealer for trust + credit; Fixxly for **speed** |
| **Helper sent to buy** | Nearest shop; price visible |
| **Homeowner** (DIY / overseer) | HomeRun-style browse; big-box |
| **Company purchase desk** | B2B platform, not 30-min app |

### 3.2 Four contractor archetypes (for product)

| Archetype | Job pattern | Sites/week | Materials pattern | Retention driver |
|-----------|-------------|------------|-------------------|------------------|
| **A. Daily repair mistri** | 1–3 small jobs/day, **new address often** | 8–15 | Small urgent SKUs; **customer pays** | **Speed + price + stock** on route; **not phase kit** |
| **B. Phase renovator** | One site **5–30 days** | 1–3 active | **Phased BOM**; 2–6 orders/site | **Job memory + next phase** ← Agla Phase |
| **C. Subcontractor specialist** | Plumber only on others’ sites | Varies | List from main contractor | Dealer / main contractor controls |
| **D. Micro-contractor + helpers** | Mix repair + small projects | Varies | Helper sent to buy | WhatsApp to dealer; voice lists |

**Your intuition is right:** Many contractors are **Archetype A** — **new job / new materials daily**, client pays. **Agla Phase Kit is for Archetype B** (and part of D when on a multi-day site).

## 4. Small jobs (&lt;1 day) vs large jobs (&gt;1 day) — best available view

**There is no authoritative India stat** for “% of contractor material orders by job duration.” Below is a **reasoned model** from proxies — **state assumptions clearly** in your submission.

### 4.1 Proxies

| Proxy | What it suggests |
|-------|------------------|
| Plumbing = **“maintenance never stops”**, steady repair income ([Digital Labour Chowk](https://digitallabourchowk.com/2026/04/06/electrician-vs-plumber-vs-mason/)) | **High frequency** of small jobs |
| Home remodeling market: **major renovations largest**; **minor remodels fastest growing** ([Deep Market Insights](https://deepmarketinsights.com/vista/insights/home-remodeling-market/india)) | **₹** skews to multi-day projects |
| Bathroom reno **₹60k–₹2.5L** total; materials **40–60%** of work | Multi-day, multi-trip materials ([Tata Capital](https://www.tatacapital.com/blog/loan-for-home/how-much-does-it-cost-to-renovate-a-home/)) |
| Individual plumber **₹600–1,200/day**; contractor **₹800–1,500/day** | Small jobs = labour-bound; materials trip separate ([Building & Interiors](https://buildingandinteriors.com/plumbing-cost-in-india-2026-price-breakdown-services-material-rates/)) |

### 4.2 Model: share of **contractor material trips** (not GMV)

| Job type | Duration | Est. % of **trips** | Est. % of **material ₹** | Typical basket | Fixxly fit |
|--------|----------|---------------------|--------------------------|----------------|------------|
| Emergency repair (leak, choke, tap) | **2–6 hrs** | **45–55%** | **15–25%** | ₹500–2,500 | **Excellent** (30 min) |
| Scheduled small install (WC, heater) | **1 day** | **20–30%** | **15–20%** | ₹2k–8k | **Strong** |
| Room / bath phase work | **3–14 days** | **15–25%** | **35–45%** | ₹5k–25k / phase | **Strong** + repeat |
| Full home / multi-trade project | **15–90 days** | **5–10%** | **25–40%** | Large, phased | Partial (not bulk cement) |

**Interpretation:**

- **By trip count**, majority can be **“today’s new job”** → **Agla Phase weak fit**.
- **By material rupees**, **multi-day jobs matter more** for GMV and **14-day retention metric**.
- Assignment metric (**second order in 14 days**) is **aligned with Archetype B**, **misaligned with Archetype A** (next job may be different site — retention = **platform habit**, not **phase**).

### 4.3 Model: share of **contractors** (active urban)

| Type | Est. % contractors | Primary retention mechanic |
|------|-------------------|----------------------------|
| Mostly daily small jobs (A) | **50–65%** | Favourites, “last order”, speed, dealer parity |
| Mix small + project (D) | **25–35%** | Both urgent + phase |
| Mostly project-based (B) | **10–20%** | **Agla Phase Kit** |

## 6. Implications for retention & Agla Phase Kit

### 6.1 Why your skepticism is valid

| Your point | Research backing |
|------------|------------------|
| New job every day | **Archetype A** = large share of trips; customer often pays |
| Agla Phase not always needed | Phase logic applies to **~15–25% of trips**, **~10–20% of contractors** (model above) |
| Retention ≠ one feature | **Segment** metrics: D14 for **multi-SKU project cohort** only |

### 6.2 Revised retention logic (two tracks)

| Track | For whom | Core mechanic | Metric |
|-------|----------|---------------|--------|
| **Track 1 — Urgent default** | Archetype A, daily jobs | 30-min + in-stock staples + reorder last basket + Hindi search | Orders / contractor / week |
| **Track 2 — Agla Phase** | Archetype B, same site 3–14d | Phase kit + stock gate | D14 reorder **per active job** |

**Week-2 retention (assignment)** blends both — a good HoP **splits the metric** in the write-up.

### 6.3 What to say in Part 1 (intellectual honesty)

> “40% no second order in 14d mixes **(i)** contractors whose next need is a **different site**, **(ii)** those still on the **same site** but buying from **dealers**, **(iii)** those who **couldn’t find SKUs**, and **(iv)** **no need yet**. Agla Phase Kit targets **(ii)** on **multi-day jobs**; Track 1 urgent reorder targets **(i)**. Pre-launch, we size **B** as ~15–25% of trips but **35–45%** of phase-material ₹.”
